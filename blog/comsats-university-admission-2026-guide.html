COMSATS University Admission 2026: Fee Structure, Merit Formula, NTS NAT & All 7 Campuses

COMSATS University Islamabad (CUI) is one of Pakistan's most applied-to universities — a public-sector institution with seven campuses, roughly 8,000 seats per year, and a reputation built on computer science, engineering and research. If you are considering COMSATS for Fall 2026, this guide covers everything: fees, the exact merit formula, the NTS NAT requirement, scholarships, and how the campuses differ.

How COMSATS Merit Is Calculated

For undergraduate admissions, COMSATS uses this weightage:

  • NTS NAT (or CUI test): 50%
  • FSc / Intermediate: 40%
  • Matric: 10%

Example: a student with 85% in Matric, 80% in FSc and 65 in NAT gets an aggregate of (8.5 + 32 + 32.5) = 73%. Minimum eligibility sits around 60%, but in-demand programs — especially BS Computer Science and BS Software Engineering at Islamabad campus — close significantly higher. Smaller campuses (Vehari, Sahiwal, Attock) close lower, which is the single most useful lever if your aggregate is borderline: same degree, same university name on your transcript, lower closing merit.

For MS programs the weightage changes to: 40% GAT General + 25% BS result + 20% interview + 10% Intermediate + 5% Matric.

The NTS NAT Requirement

COMSATS accepts the NTS NAT (National Aptitude Test) for BS admissions — you can sit any NAT attempt whose result is valid at the time of application, so it is worth attempting NAT early and improving your score with a second attempt if needed. GAT General is required for MS, GAT Subject for PhD. Since NAT is 50% of your merit, a focused month of past-paper practice moves your aggregate more than anything else you can do at this stage.

COMSATS vs the Alternatives

If you are choosing between COMSATS and FAST for computing: FAST's brand in CS is stronger in the job market, but COMSATS offers more seats, more campuses and comparable curriculum at a similar fee. Against public giants like NUST, COMSATS is easier to get into (NAT vs NET, lower closing merit) at a somewhat higher fee than NUST's subsidised programs. Bottom Line

COMSATS is arguably Pakistan's best value-for-merit computing university: genuinely good CS education, 8,000 seats, and campus options that let students with a 65–75% aggregate still get in.
